Character Anatomy & Capabilities
================================

In [Game Name], characters are defined by the intersection of their history, their current focus, and the weight of their destiny. To represent this, a character is composed of several distinct mechanical layers:

1. **Qualities:** Narrative descriptors of identity and training.
2. **Attributes:** Raw physical and mental potential.
3. **Traits & Statuses:** Persistent or temporary modifiers to capabilities.
4. **Abilities:** Specialized techniques and powers.
5. **Vital Resources:** Dwindling pools used to power actions.
6. **Armor:** External protection and damage reduction.
7. **Equipment:** Gear that is worn or readily accessible.
8. **Inventory & Encumbrance:** The burden of carrying gear.

1. Qualities
------------

A character's background, training, and personality are represented by **Quality Tags**. These are descriptive phrases that carry significant narrative weight and directly influence the outcome of rolls through **The Quality Edge**. (See: :ref:`quality_edge`)

**Quality Tags** are divided into three categories:

* **Identity:** Establishes who a character is (e.g., *Occult Bureau Investigator*, *Disgraced Elven Royal*).
* **Talents:** Establishes what a character knows or is trained in (e.g., *Master of Clockwork Devices*). Note that certain **Traits** can also function as Talents when their expertise is relevant. (See: :ref:`traits_talents`)
* **Compulsions:** Establishes fundamental **Imperatives** or **Flaws**—absolute rules, codes of conduct, or deep-seated urges the character must follow (e.g., *Must always be polite*, *Cannot resist a gamble*, *Must never leave a debt unpaid*).

**Compulsions** (Imperatives or Flaws) are the primary trigger for **Compelled Actions**. Whether they represent a noble principle or a detrimental weakness, these tags create narrative pressure and dramatic friction. When a character leans into their compulsion, they gain an **Intervention Point**. Alternatively, they may be forced to spend an IP to resist their nature or a personal code of conduct. (See: :ref:`compelled_actions`)

2. Attributes
-------------

**Attributes** define the character's raw, inherent capabilities. They are the primary driver of success in the 2d10 resolution engine and determine the starting values for various resource pools.

* **Strength:** Physical force and lifting capacity.
* **Endurance:** Resilience and physical stamina.
* **Agility:** Speed and reflexes.
* **Dexterity:** Precision and fine motor skills.
* **Intelligence:** Reasoning and problem-solving.
* **Willpower:** Mental fortitude and magical potential.
* **Acuity:** Perception and situational awareness.
* **Charisma:** Social persuasion and leadership.

**Game Methodology: The Attribute-Driven Bell Curve**
By using a 2d10 dice pool, the system ensures that results cluster around the mathematical average. This makes character **Attributes**—which provide a flat, static bonus—extraordinarily meaningful. While a single d20 can produce wild swings of luck, 2d10 allows an expert with a high Attribute to reliably achieve success, reinforcing the idea that a character's inherent training is the most significant factor in their success.

3. Traits & Statuses
--------------------

* **Traits:** Persistent characteristics that represent specialized training, inherent qualities, or long-term growth (e.g., *Fleet-Footed*, *High Pain Tolerance*). Traits are often represented by **Reference Cards** for quick consultation during play. (See: :ref:`traits`)
* **Statuses:** Temporary conditions (Short-term or Long-term) that affect capabilities, often triggered by damage, environmental effects, or the resolution of actions (e.g., *Stunned*, *Bleeding*, *Inspired*). Statuses are often represented by **Reference Cards** for quick consultation during play. (See: :ref:`statuses`)

Both **Traits** and **Statuses** can serve as triggers for **Compelled Actions**, representing internal quirks or external conditions that override a character's immediate control. (See: :ref:`compelled_actions`)

**Statuses as Consequences**
Statuses are a primary mechanical tool for representing the lasting impact of an action's success or failure. A **Perfect** success might reward a character with a positive status (e.g., *Empowered* or *Inspired*), while a **Disaster** or **Fumble** might inflict a negative status (e.g., *Rattled*, *Exposed*, or *Bleeding*).

For convenience, it is recommended to keep Traits and Statuses on reference cards for quick consultation during play.

4. Abilities
------------

**Abilities** are active specialized techniques, maneuvers, or powers that require a conscious expenditure of resources (**Stamina**, **IP**, or **Essence**). Abilities are often represented by **Reference Cards**, allowing players to build a dynamic "hand" of combat and utility options. (See: :ref:`abilities`)

**Game Methodology: Abilities vs. Narrative Actions**
In this system, any player can describe their character performing a **Narrative Action** (e.g., "I climb the wall"). These are resolved through simple **Action Rolls**. **Abilities**, however, are pre-defined mechanical levers that often allow a character to bypass normal rules, target multiple foes, or trigger unique effects like Statuses. This allows for a clear distinction between common expertise and specialized mastery.

For convenience, it is recommended to keep Abilities on reference cards for quick consultation during play.

5. Vital Resources
------------------
A character's vitality is measured across four distinct pools, each representing a different layer of their ability to endure, exert influence, and survive the trials of their journey.

**Spending vs. Reserving**

*   **Spending:** The most common form of expenditure where points are removed from a pool to trigger an immediate effect. Spent resources can be recovered through standard rests.
*   **Reserving:** Points are "locked" to sustain a persistent Trait, Status, or Ability. While a resource is reserved, it cannot be spent and cannot be recovered until the sustaining effect ends. This effectively lowers the pool's usable maximum.

.. _vital_resources_health:

Health
~~~~~~
**Health** represents the character's physical integrity and threshold of existence. It is the ultimate measure of survival; reaching 0 results in being **Incapacitated** and potentially dying. Players must carefully manage this pool as standard healing is governed by the **Health Recovery Cap**, requiring specialized care or a **Recovery Rest** to mend deep trauma. (See: :ref:`rest_actions`)

*   **Spending:** Depleted by damage in combat, environmental hazards, or specialized blood-magic costs.
*   **Reserving:** Locked to sustain powerful physical transformations or persistent life-linked effects.

The Health Recovery Cap
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^
The **Health Recovery Cap** is a critical threshold that separates "surface trauma" (scratches, bruises, and spiritual fatigue) from "deep trauma" (internal injuries and structural damage).

*   **Default Value:** By default, the cap is set at **50% of the character's maximum Health (rounded down)**.
*   **Effect of Reservation:** The Cap is always calculated based on the character's *current effective maximum* (Maximum Health minus all currently Reserved Health).
*   **Mechanical Effect:** Standard healing methods—including **Long Rests**—cannot restore Health beyond this cap. Only specialized methods or a **Recovery Rest** can bypass or remove this limit. (See: :ref:`rest_actions`)

Stamina
~~~~~~~
**Stamina** represents the character's physical breath, muscle fatigue, and immediate **Will**. It is the primary fuel for physical exertion. Depleting Stamina leaves a character vulnerable and tactically limited.

*   **Spending:** Used to activate physical Abilities, defend against attacks, or exert extreme effort on narrative actions.
*   **Reserving:** Used to maintain physical stances, sustained physical effort, or heavy equipment burdens.

Essence
~~~~~~~
**Essence** represents the character's mental bandwidth, spiritual focus, and mystical capacity. It is the fuel for supernatural interactions, specialized spellcasting, and resisting mental status effects. High Essence is critical for resolving tasks requiring deep mental or spiritual concentration.

*   **Spending:** Used for immediate magical spells, specialized mental feats, or to resist psychic intrusion.
*   **Reserving:** Used to maintain active magical concentrations, sustained enchantments, or spiritual wards.

Intervention Points (IP)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
**Intervention Points (IP)** represents the character's **Fate**—their authorial agency and the collective will of the group acting upon the meta-narrative. Unlike other resources, IPs allow a player to influence the world directly or support allies when standard mechanics fall short. (See: :ref:`modifying_action_rolls`)

*   **Spending:** Used for supporting others, narrative retcons, or providing a "Universal Safety Net."
*   **Generation:** Primarily generated through the **Compelled Action** system (leaning into flaws).

**Game Methodology: The Strategic Resource Economy**
Resources are the primary currency of the game. Every significant action requires a deliberate investment. This shifts the focus from simply "rolling to see if I win" to a strategic question: "Is this action worth the cost?" Managing the four resources is a delicate balancing act between physical survival (**Health**), tactical effort (**Stamina**), supernatural focus (**Essence**), and narrative destiny (**IP**).

6. Armor
--------
**Armor** provides a character with a "static buffer" against incoming trauma, reducing the number of points lost from their **Vital Resources** when they suffer a hit.

*   **Physical Armor:** Protects against kinetic force, blades, and blunt trauma. It provides **Physical Damage Reduction (PDR)**.
*   **Ephemeral Armor:** Protects against magical, spiritual, or mental trauma. It provides **Ephemeral Damage Reduction (EDR)**.

**Damage Reduction (DR)**
When a character suffers damage, their relevant Armor value is subtracted from the total before it affects their pools. Some specialized attacks or abilities may **Bypass** or **Sunder** Armor, ignoring this reduction entirely.

7. Equipment
------------
**Equipment** represents the items that are currently worn or readily accessible on the character's person. These items determine the character's immediate tactical capabilities. Every character possesses a set of functional **Equipment Slots**, regardless of their specific physiology. Items used as equipment are represented by **Reference Cards**. (See: :ref:`items`)

A character's standard equipment capacity is defined by the following slots:

*   **Head (1):** Helmets, hoods, or sensory enhancements.
*   **Body (1):** Primary armor, robes, or protective outer-wear.
*   **Arms (1):** Bracers, gauntlets, gloves, or protective rings.
*   **Legs (1):** Boots, greaves, or reinforced locomotion-wear.
*   **Trinket (2):** Small, focused items such as charms, amulets, or jewelry.
*   **Weapon/Tool (2):** Dedicated tools for interaction, including weapons, mystical catalysts, and specialized apparatuses.

**Equipping Items**
Only items assigned to these slots provide persistent mechanical effects or can be used as a free part of an action. While characters may carry many other items, they must be "Equipped" into a slot to be fully functional. (Note: All items implicitly act as narrative tools where appropriate.)

.. _encumbrance:

8. Inventory & Encumbrance
--------------------------
**Inventory** represents all items carried by the character that are not currently equipped in an active slot. Every item has an **Encumbrance Point (EP)** value reflecting its weight, bulk, and difficulty to transport. (See: :ref:`items`)

**Encumbrance Points (EP)**
The total burden of a character is the sum of the EP values of **all** carried items. This includes gear currently equipped in **Equipment Slots** as well as items stowed in the **Inventory**.

*   **Standard Capacity:** A character's standard carrying capacity is determined by their physical and mental strength (typically a base value modified by attributes).
*   **Minor Items:** Small, negligible items do not contribute to EP unless carried in significant bulk.

**Encumbrance & Exhaustion**
If a character's total EP exceeds their standard capacity, they suffer from physical and mental strain.

*   **Mechanical Effect:** For every EP beyond their capacity, the character must **Reserve 1 Stamina**. This represents the ongoing physical toll of maintaining their gear and the mental fatigue of managing a complex load.
*   **Overburdened:** If a character's Stamina pool is fully reserved or depleted by their EP burden, they become **Overburdened**. In this state, they cannot take any **Action Rolls** involving physical movement or complex agility until they drop enough weight to free at least 1 point of Stamina.
