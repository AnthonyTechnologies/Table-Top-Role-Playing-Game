Statuses
========

**Statuses** are temporary dynamic conditions that represent the immediate narrative and mechanical impact of actions, environmental hazards, or supernatural effects. Unlike **Traits**, which are persistent parts of a character's identity, Statuses represent the "ripple effects" of the story and are expected to change frequently during play.

For physical play, Statuses are intended to be represented by **Reference Cards**, allowing the GM and players to quickly track active conditions without excessive bookkeeping.

The Anatomy of a Status
-----------------------

Each status is defined by several key components, ensuring that every shift in a character's condition has clear narrative stakes and mechanical teeth.

1. **Name**
~~~~~~~~~~~
The unique title of the status (e.g., *Inspired*, *Bleeding*, *Rattled*, *Stunned*).

2. **Category (Severity)**
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
The **Category** of a status represents its overall impact on a character's capabilities and the difficulty of its resolution:

*   **Minor:** Negligible effects that create slight narrative friction.
*   **Moderate:** Significant conditions that require tactical adjustment.
*   **Major:** Severe impacts that can cripple a character's core functions.
*   **Critical:** Dire states that represent life-threatening hazards or extreme narrative shifts.

3. **Quality Tags**
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Statuses possess their own **Quality Tags**—descriptive keywords that act as tangible qualities of the condition (e.g., *[Physical]*, *[Mental]*, *[Bleeding]*). These tags can be invoked during an **Action Roll** via **The Quality Edge**. For example, a character who is *[Inspired]* might invoke that quality to gain an edge on a difficult roll, while an enemy might invoke a character's *[Rattled]* status to gain an edge against them. (See: :ref:`quality_edge`)

4. **Description**
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
A short narrative explanation of what the status represents and how it manifests in the fiction.

5. **Activation & Requirements**
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Defines the specific triggers or conditions under which the status's mechanical effects manifest. A character may possess a status that remains dormant until a certain action is taken, a resource is expended, or an environmental threshold is met.

6. **Mechanical Effect**
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
The specific rules modification imposed by the status once activated. This usually involves penalties to **Attributes**, restrictions on **Abilities**, or increased resource costs. Positive statuses provide bonuses or unique narrative permissions.

7. **Resolution**
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Defines how and when the status is removed. The resolution of a status falls into three primary types:

*   **Automatic:** The status resolves itself after a set duration (e.g., *End of Turn*, *End of Scene*).
*   **Reactive:** The status persists until a specific action is taken or a resource is expended (e.g., *Requires a successful First Aid roll*, *Dispelled by a Short Rest*).
*   **Persistent:** The status does not resolve on its own and requires a significant narrative effort or specific GM-defined criteria to overcome.

.. _statuses_as_consequences:

Statuses as Consequences
------------------------
As defined in the Outcome Brackets, **Statuses** are the primary way a GM represents the consequences of successes and failures. A **Perfect** success should feel earned through a positive status, while a **Disaster** or **Fumble** should have a lingering mechanical sting through a negative one. (See: :ref:`outcome_brackets`)
