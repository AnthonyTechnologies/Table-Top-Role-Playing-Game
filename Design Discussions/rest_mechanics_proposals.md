# Proposals: Enhancing Tactical Incentives for Rests

This document outlines options for strengthening the tactical distinction between "Long Rests" and "Recovery Rests."

## Option 1: The "Deep Trauma" Penalty (Mechanical Push)

To make the **Recovery Rest** essential, being below the **50% Health Recovery Cap** should carry a persistent mechanical burden. A Long Rest gets a character *to* the cap, but they remain "wounded" until they heal fully.

*   **The Weight of Wounds:** While a character's Health is at or below 50% of their maximum, they suffer **Disfavor** on all physical Action Rolls (Brawn, Agility, Dexterity) to represent lingering injury and pain.
*   **Tactical Result:** Players will avoid staying in "Deep Trauma" because it makes them significantly less effective in future encounters.

## Option 2: Status-Based Recovery (Functional Distinction)

Differentiate the *types* of complications each rest can handle.

*   **Short Rest:** Clears only immediate, transient effects (e.g., *Winded*).
*   **Long Rest:** Clears all **Short-term Statuses**.
*   **Recovery Rest:** Clears all **Short-term** and **Long-term Statuses**.
*   **Tactical Result:** If a character has a *Broken Arm* or *Concussion* (Long-term Statuses), a Long Rest is insufficient. They *must* take a Recovery Rest to return to functional health.

## Option 3: Peak Condition Bonus (Mechanical Pull)

Reward the player for achieving 100% Health, which is difficult without a **Recovery Rest**.

*   **Rested & Ready:** If a character begins their day at 100% Health after a **Recovery Rest**, they gain **Favor** on the first **Action Roll** of the next narrative scene.
*   **Tactical Result:** Players have a positive incentive to seek full healing rather than just "getting by" on a Long Rest.

## Recommendation

I suggest combining **Option 1 (Deep Trauma Penalty)** with **Option 2 (Long-term Statuses)**. This creates a clear hierarchy:
1.  **Long Rest** is for survival (stopping the bleeding).
2.  **Recovery Rest** is for effectiveness (returning to peak performance and clearing lasting trauma).
