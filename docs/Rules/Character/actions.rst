Actions
=======

Characters in [Game Name] will constantly interact with the world, face obstacles, and clash with adversaries. These interactions are initiated through **Actions**. Anything a character does is considered an **Action**, but only under certain conditions do **Actions** require **Resolution Rolls** or **Resource** expenditure to determine the outcome.

1. Narrative Actions
--------------------

Players may attempt any action they can describe within the fiction. For simple actions, the narrative may continue without resource expenditure or a roll. However, it is highly encouraged for most significant or strenuous actions that the playgroup evaluate if resources should be expended and if a roll is required to resolve the action.

First, the playgroup must determine if and how resources should be expended to resolve the action. 

* **Relevant Techniques:** The acting player may suggest using a **Technique** that describes the action. If the GM agrees, the character may follow the conditions and resource expenditures (such as **Stamina** or **Essence**) defined by that Technique.
* **GM-Defined Expenditure:** If an action does not clearly pertain to a specific Technique, or if the player chooses not to use one, the GM decides which resources the character must expend to attempt the task. This choice is based on the nature of the challenge and may include **Stamina**, **Essence**, **Health**, **Intervention**, or other context-appropriate costs.
* **Additional Costs:** The GM may request additional resource expenditure even when an existing Technique fits the action, representing unforeseen complications, extreme effort, or the high stakes of the specific moment.

Once the action is confirmed and any required expenditures are determined, the playgroup may decide if an **Action Roll** (a type of **Ramification Roll**) is required to resolve the outcome.

See section :ref:`action_rolls` on when and how to roll.

.. container:: deemphasized

    **Game Methodology: Strategic Resource Expenditure**

    By encouraging resource expenditure (such as **Stamina** or **Essence**) before a roll is even considered, the system ensures that significant actions carry a weight beyond random chance. This methodology prevents "dice-rolling fatigue" where players attempt tasks repeatedly without consequence. Instead, it shifts the focus from a binary pass/fail state to a strategic question of value. Frequent expenditure keeps the action economy flowing, ensuring that even when a roll is not required, the decision to act remains a meaningful investment of the character's finite energy. This approach builds natural tension as players weigh immediate goals against their dwindling long-term resources.

3. Rest Actions
---------------
Characters need periods of respite to recover their spent resources. Restoration is divided into three distinct types of rest, each representing a different level of recuperation.

Short Rest
~~~~~~~~~~
A brief period of downtime (e.g., catching one's breath, a quick meal, or a moment of reflection).

*   **Recovery:** Restores all **Stamina** and **Essence** pools to their maximum.
*   **Health:** Does not restore **Health** unless the character expends a permissive item (like a bandage) or uses a specialized Technique.
*   **Statuses:** Only clears transient or immediate narrative effects (e.g., *Winded*).

Long Rest
~~~~~~~~~
An extended period of sleep or safety (e.g., a night at an inn, a secure camp, or a day of light travel).

*   **Recovery:** Restores all **Stamina** and **Essence** pools to their maximum.
*   **Health:** Restores **Health** up to the **Health Recovery Cap**.
*   **Statuses:** Clears all **Short-term Statuses**.

Recovery Rest
~~~~~~~~~~~~~
A significant period of treatment and deep recuperation (e.g., several days of bedrest in a sanctuary or under professional medical care).

*   **Recovery:** Restores all **Health**, **Stamina**, and **Essence** pools to their absolute maximum.
*   **Bypassing the Cap:** This is the primary mundane method for restoring Health beyond the **Health Recovery Cap**.
*   **Statuses:** Clears all **Short-term** and **Long-term Statuses**.

.. container:: deemphasized

    **Game Methodology: The Health Recovery Cap**

    In [Game Name], Health restored up to the **Health Recovery Cap** represents the mending of "surface trauma"—scratches, bruises, and spiritual exhaustion that can be mended with a night's sleep. Health missing beyond this cap represents "deep trauma"—internal injuries and structural damage that require time, specialized care, or rare mystical intervention. This methodology ensures that combat has lasting narrative consequences, preventing characters from simply "sleeping off" a near-fatal wound without dedicated medical attention or a significant period of downtime. (See: :ref:`character_resources_health`)
