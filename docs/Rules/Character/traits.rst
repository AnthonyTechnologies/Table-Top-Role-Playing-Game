.. _traits:

Traits
======

**Traits** are persistent specialized capabilities that represent a character's long-term training, inherent qualities, or hard-won experience. Unlike **Statuses**, which are temporary conditions, Traits are fundamental parts of a character's identity that provide consistent mechanical and narrative advantages.

For physical play, Traits are intended to be represented by **Reference Cards**, allowing players to quickly consult their capabilities and swap them as they progress or change focus.

The Anatomy of a Trait
----------------------

Each trait is defined by several key components, ensuring that every specialized capability has a clear narrative context and mechanical weight.

1. **Name**
~~~~~~~~~~~
The unique title of the trait (e.g., *Fleet-Footed*, *Sharp-Eyed*, *Iron Skin*).

2. **Category (Power Level)**
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
The **Category** of a trait represents its complexity, rarity, and overall power level. This aligns with the game's standard skill progression:

*   **Novice:** Common, foundational traits accessible to most characters.
*   **Adept:** Specialized traits requiring significant training or experience.
*   **Master:** Rare, highly powerful traits representing peak competence.
*   **Grandmaster:** Legendary capabilities that define the absolute limits of mortal potential.

3. **Persona Tags**
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Traits possess their own **Persona Tags**—descriptive keywords that act as tangible qualities of the trait itself (e.g., *[Agile]*, *[Resilient]*, *[Arcane]*). These tags can be invoked during an **Action Roll** in the same way as character persona, allowing the character to gain **Persona's Edge** if the trait's nature is relevant to the task. (See: :ref:`persona_edge`)

4. **Description**
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
A short narrative explanation of what the trait represents in the world and how it manifests in the character's actions.

5. **Activation & Requirements**
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Defines exactly **when** and **how** the trait applies. Some traits are always active (Passive), while others require specific narrative conditions, a minimum score in a specific **Attribute**, or the expenditure of a resource (**Stamina** or **Essence**).

**Persona Tag Requirements:** Many traits include **Persona Tag Requirements**, meaning they only activate if the character possesses specific **Identity** or **Talent** tags. This represents how a character’s background or training acts as a prerequisite for their specialized capabilities. **Note:** Any Persona Tag used as a requirement to activate a trait—or any tag of the same type (e.g., a higher tier of the same skill, as determined by the GM)—cannot also be used to gain **Persona's Edge** for that specific action.

6. **Effect**
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
The core mechanical benefit of the trait. This may include:

*   **Resource Bonuses:** Flat increases to **Maximum Health**, **Stamina**, **Essence**, or **Encumbrance Capacity**. (See: :ref:`characters`)
*   **Attribute Bonuses:** Permanent increases to a character's raw potential.
*   **Narrative Permissions:** Unique ways to interact with the world or bypass standard rules in specific situations.

.. _traits_talents:

Traits as Talents
-----------------
Beyond their specific mechanical benefits, **Traits** are functionally treated as character **Talents**. Because a trait represents a high degree of specialized expertise or an inherent quality, its name and nature act as a permanent **Persona Tag**.

When a character performs an action where their trait’s specialized expertise is relevant, the player can invoke the trait as a Talent to gain **Persona's Edge**. This allows characters with specialized traits to reliably succeed in their niche even when not using a specific mechanical Technique. (See: :ref:`techniques`)

.. _traits_consequences:

Traits as Consequences
----------------------
In some cases, a character may acquire a trait as a permanent narrative reward for an extraordinary **Miracle** or as a lasting scar from a devastating **Catastrophe**. Long-term development through play often manifests as the gaining or upgrading of traits, reflecting the character's growth throughout their journey.
