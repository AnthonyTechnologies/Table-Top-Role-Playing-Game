.. _persona:

Persona
=======

A character's background, training, and personality are represented by **Persona Tags**. These are descriptive phrases that carry significant narrative weight and directly influence the outcome of rolls through **Persona's Edge**. (See: :ref:`persona_edge`)

**Persona Tags** are divided into three categories:

*   **Identity:** Establishes who a character is (e.g., *Occult Bureau Investigator*, *Disgraced Elven Royal*).
*   **Talents:** Establishes what a character knows or is trained in (e.g., *Master of Clockwork Devices*). Note that certain **Traits** can also function as Talents when their expertise is relevant. (See: :ref:`traits_talents`)
*   **Compulsions:** Establishes fundamental **Imperatives** or **Flaws**—absolute rules, codes of conduct, or deep-seated urges the character must follow (e.g., *Must always be polite*, *Cannot resist a gamble*, *Must never leave a debt unpaid*).

.. _compelled_actions:

Compelled Actions
-----------------

A character's **Compulsions** (Imperatives or Flaws) are the primary trigger for **Compelled Actions**. Whether they represent a noble principle or a detrimental weakness, these tags create narrative pressure and dramatic friction. When a character leans into their compulsion, they gain **Intervention**. Alternatively, they may be forced to spend **Intervention** to resist their nature or a personal code of conduct.

A **Compelled Action** occurs when the GM — or a player with the GM's permission — triggers a character's **Compulsions**, **Traits**, or **Statuses** to force a specific narrative or mechanical reaction. This represents the character's internal flaws or external conditions overriding their immediate tactical preferences.

**The Compel Process**
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

When the GM initiates a compel:

*   **The Action:** The character must perform a specific action described by the GM, which may require resource expenditure and/or an **Action Roll**.
*   **Accepting the Compel:** If the player accepts the complication and resolves the situation — either through roleplay or a successful/failed roll — the GM must immediately award the player **1 Intervention**.
*   **Resisting the Compel:** If the player wishes to avoid the compelled action, they must spend **1 Intervention** to suppress their impulse or overcome the condition. If they cannot pay this cost, they must accept the compel.

.. container:: deemphasized

    **Game Methodology: Fueling the Fates**

    Compelled Actions create a self-sustaining narrative loop. By allowing the GM to introduce complications based on character flaws, the player gains the meta-narrative currency (**Intervention**) needed to exert agency elsewhere. This methodology encourages players to lean into interesting failures and internal conflicts, knowing that their willingness to suffer narratively today will provide the "fate" needed to succeed heroically tomorrow.
