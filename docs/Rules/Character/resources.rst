.. _character_resources:

Character Resources
===================

A character's vitality is measured across four distinct pools, each representing a different layer of their capability to endure, exert influence, and survive the trials of their journey.

**Spending vs. Reserving**
--------------------------

*   **Spending:** The most common form of expenditure where points are removed from a pool to trigger an immediate effect. Spent resources can be recovered through standard rests.
*   **Reserving:** Points are "locked" to sustain a persistent Trait, Status, or Technique. While a resource is reserved, it cannot be spent and cannot be recovered until the sustaining effect ends. This effectively lowers the pool's usable maximum.

It is highly recommended to use colored poker chips to track resource totals. This allows for quick, tactile manipulation during dynamic scenes. For accessibility, chips could be marked or textured to assist colorblind players. Players should record their current totals on their character sheet at the end of each session to ensure continuity.

**Baseline Resource Pools**
---------------------------
Every character begins their journey with a functional baseline for their primary resources. These values represent a "standard" level of endurance and focus for a novice adventurer:

*   **Max Health:** 50
*   **Max Stamina:** 10
*   **Max Essence:** 10

**Scaling Resources**
---------------------
Unlike **Action Rolls**, which scale with **Attributes**, resource pools are modular. A character's maximum capacity for survival and exertion is increased through:

*   **Persona Tags:** Certain **Identity** or **Talent** tags (e.g., *[Vanguard]*, *[Iron-Willed]*) may provide permanent bonuses to specific pools.
*   **Traits:** Many **Traits** (e.g., *Sturdy*, *Deep Breath*, *Arcane Mind*) are designed specifically to expand a character's resource depth. (See: :ref:`traits`)
*   **Equipment:** Specialized gear or magical items may temporarily or persistently increase maximum resource values while equipped.

.. _character_resources_health:

Health
------
**Health** represents a character's physical integrity and immediate threshold of survival. It is the ultimate measure of their presence in the world; when this pool is depleted, the character's narrative agency is suspended.

*   **Spending:** Depleted by damage in combat, environmental hazards, or specialized blood-magic costs.
*   **Reserving:** Locked to sustain powerful physical transformations or persistent life-linked effects.

The Health Recovery Cap
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
The **Health Recovery Cap** defines the limit of natural recovery. It separates "surface trauma" (bruises, spiritual fatigue) from "deep trauma" (structural damage and internal injuries) that requires specialized attention.

*   **Cap Value:** Set at **50% of the character's maximum Health (rounded down)**.
*   **Calculation:** Based on the character's current effective maximum (Max Health minus Reserved Health).
*   **Mechanical Effect:** Standard healing and **Long Rests** cannot restore Health beyond this cap. Only specialized methods or a **Recovery Rest** can bypass or remove this limit. (See: :ref:`rest_actions`)

.. _incapacitated_state:

Incapacitated & Death
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
When a character's **Health** reaches 0, they immediately enter the **Incapacitated** state. This is a critical threshold where the character's narrative agency is suspended as they fight for survival.

**The Incapacitated State**

While a character is **Incapacitated**, the following rules apply:

*   **Unaware:** The character is completely unconscious and unaware of their surroundings or currently unfolding events.
*   **No Actions:** The character cannot take any actions, including narrative maneuvers, movement, or the activation of **Techniques**.
*   **Vulnerability:** The character is at constant risk of permanent injury or death and must rely on their own resilience or the help of allies.

**Resolution:** A character remains in this state until their Health is restored to at least 1, or they stabilize following a **Crisis Roll**.

At the start of their turn, an **Incapacitated** character must make a **Crisis Roll** to determine if they stabilize or succumb to their wounds. (See: :ref:`crisis_roll`)

Stamina
-------
**Stamina** represents the character's physical breath, muscle fatigue, and immediate **Will**. It is the primary fuel for physical exertion. Depleting Stamina leaves a character vulnerable and tactically limited.

*   **Spending:** Used to activate physical Techniques, defend against attacks, or exert extreme effort on narrative actions.
*   **Reserving:** Used to maintain physical stances, sustained physical effort, or heavy equipment burdens.

Essence
-------
**Essence** represents the character's mental bandwidth, spiritual focus, and mystical capacity. It is the fuel for supernatural interactions, specialized spellcasting, and resisting mental status effects. High Essence is critical for resolving tasks requiring deep mental or spiritual concentration.

*   **Spending:** Used for immediate magical spells, specialized mental feats, or to resist psychic intrusion.
*   **Reserving:** Used to maintain active magical concentrations, sustained enchantments, or spiritual wards.

Intervention
------------
**Intervention** represents the character's fate — their authorial agency and the collective will of the group acting upon the meta-narrative. Unlike other resources, **Intervention** allows a player to influence the world directly or support allies when standard mechanics fall short. (See: :ref:`modifying_action_rolls`)

*   **Generation:** Primarily generated through the **Compelled Action** system (leaning into **Compulsions**).

**Uses:**

*   **Intervention Shift:** A player may spend **1 Intervention** to shift their own **Action Roll** resolution bracket either up or down one.
*   **Team Up-Shift:** A player may spend **1 Intervention** to assist a teammate during an **Action Roll** to shift their resolution bracket up one.
*   **Narrative Retcon:** A player may spend **1 Intervention** to introduce a minor, favorable detail into the current scene (e.g., finding a useful tool or a convenient distraction).
*   **Narrative Flashback:** A player may spend **1 Intervention** to establish a past preparation that provides a current advantage (e.g., having a specific tool or prior knowledge).
*   **The Universal Safety Net:** A player may spend **1 Intervention** to gain a **Persona Shift** for an action where they lack a relevant **Persona Tag**.
*   **Traits & Techniques:** Some **Traits** and **Techniques** may require the expenditure of **Intervention** to activate.

.. container:: deemphasized

    **Game Methodology: The Strategic Resource Economy**

    Character Resources are the primary agency currencies of the game. Every significant action requires a deliberate investment. This shifts the focus from simply "rolling to see if I win" to a strategic question: "Is this action worth the cost?" Managing the four resources is a delicate balancing act between physical survival (**Health**), tactical effort (**Stamina**), supernatural focus (**Essence**), and narrative destiny (**Intervention**).
