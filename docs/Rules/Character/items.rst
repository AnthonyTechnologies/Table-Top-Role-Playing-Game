.. _items:

Items
=====

**Items** are tangible assets, tools, and gear that characters carry, use, and trade during their journey. Every item in the world—from a simple loaf of bread to a legendary masterwork blade—is defined by its physical properties and narrative significance.

For physical play, Items are intended to be represented by **Reference Cards**, allowing players to easily manage their inventory, track encumbrance, and swap equipment without constant rewriting on a character sheet.

The Anatomy of an Item
----------------------

Each item is defined by several key components, ensuring that every physical asset has a clear mechanical footprint and narrative context.

1. **Name**
~~~~~~~~~~~
The unique title of the item (e.g., *Steel Broadsword*, *Traveler's Cloak*, *Superior Lockpicks*).

2. **Category (Quality/Rarity)**
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
The **Category** of an item represents its craftsmanship, rarity, and overall effectiveness:

*   **Common:** Mass-produced or simple items accessible to everyone.
*   **Masterwork:** Finely crafted gear that provides superior performance.
*   **Rare:** Hard-to-find items with unique properties or historical significance.
*   **Relic:** Legendary objects of immense power or ancient origin.

3. **Value**
~~~~~~~~~~~~
The economic worth of the item in the game's standard currency. This represents the item's baseline price for trading, selling, or bribing.

4. **Encumbrance (EP)**
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
The physical burden of carrying the item. Every item has an **Encumbrance Point (EP)** value that contributes to the character's total load. Small or light items may be "Negligible" (0 EP), while heavy gear can impose significant pressure. (See: :ref:`encumbrance`)

5. **Type & Slot Type**
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Defines how the item is used or carried:

*   **Consumable:** An item that is destroyed or depleted after use (e.g., *Healing Salve*, *Rations*).
*   **Utility:** General tools and gear that don't occupy a specific equipment slot.
*   **Equipment:** Items that must be "active" to provide their benefit. Equipment occupies one of the following **Equipment Slots**:
    *   **Head:** Helmets, cowls, or headbands. (1 Slot)
    *   **Body:** Armor, robes, or protective outer-wear. (1 Slot)
    *   *   **Arms:** Bracers, gauntlets, gloves, or protective rings. (1 Slot)
    *   *   **Legs:** Boots, greaves, or reinforced footwear. (1 Slot)
    *   **Trinket:** Amulets, rings, or small charms. (2 Slots)
    *   **Weapon/Tool:** Swords, bows, hammers, or specialized kits. (2 Slots)

6. **Persona Tags**
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Items possess their own **Persona Tags**—descriptive keywords that act as tangible qualities of the object (e.g., *[Sharp]*, *[Heated]*, *[Concealable]*). These tags can be invoked during an **Action Roll** via **Persona's Edge**, representing how the tool's properties assist the character. (See: :ref:`persona_edge`)

7. **Description**
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
A short narrative explanation of the item's appearance, origin, and intended use.

8. **Activation & Requirements (Optional)**
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Some items—particularly magic gear or complex machinery—require specific conditions or **Attribute** thresholds to use correctly. For example, a heavy shield might require a certain **Brawn** score to avoid penalties.

9. **Mechanical Effect**
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
The specific rules modification provided by the item. This may include bonuses to damage, unique narrative permissions, or protection against specific types of harm.

Equipment Rules
---------------

**Equipment** represents the items that are currently worn or readily accessible on the character's person. These items determine the character's immediate tactical capabilities. Every character possesses a set of functional **Equipment Slots**, regardless of their specific physiology. Items used as equipment are represented by **Reference Cards**.

A character's standard equipment capacity is defined by the following slots:

*   **Head (1):** Helmets, hoods, or sensory enhancements.
*   **Body (1):** Primary armor, robes, or protective outer-wear.
*   **Arms (1):** Bracers, gauntlets, gloves, or protective rings.
*   **Legs (1):** Boots, greaves, or reinforced locomotion-wear.
*   **Trinket (2):** Small, focused items such as charms, amulets, or jewelry.
*   **Weapon/Tool (2):** Dedicated tools for interaction, including weapons, mystical catalysts, and specialized apparatuses.

**Equipping Items**
Only items assigned to these slots provide persistent mechanical effects or can be used as a free part of an action. While characters may carry many other items, they must be "Equipped" into a slot to be fully functional. (Note: All items implicitly act as narrative tools where appropriate.)

Inventory & Encumbrance
------------------------

**Inventory** represents all items carried by the character that are not currently equipped in an active slot. Every item has an **Encumbrance Point (EP)** value reflecting its weight, bulk, and difficulty to transport. (See: :ref:`items`)

**Encumbrance Points (EP)**
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
The total burden of a character is the sum of the EP values of **all** carried items. This includes gear currently equipped in **Equipment Slots** as well as items stowed in the **Inventory**.

*   **Standard Capacity:** A character's standard carrying capacity is a flat baseline of **10 Encumbrance Points (EP)**.
*   **Minor Items:** Small, negligible items do not contribute to EP unless carried in significant bulk.

**Encumbrance & Exhaustion**
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
If a character's total EP exceeds their standard capacity, they suffer from physical and mental strain.

*   **Mechanical Effect:** For every EP beyond their capacity, the character must **Reserve 1 Stamina**. This represents the ongoing physical toll of maintaining their gear and the mental fatigue of managing a complex load.
*   **Overburdened:** If a character's Stamina pool is fully reserved or depleted by their EP burden, they become **Overburdened**. In this state, they cannot take any **Action Rolls** involving physical movement or complex agility until they drop enough weight to free at least 1 point of Stamina.
